Privacy

How Inner Pathway handles personal information.

This notice explains how Inner Pathway Counselling collects, uses, stores and protects personal information, and the rights you have under UK data protection law.

You do not need to include detailed personal, medical or counselling information in an initial email or telephone enquiry.

1. Who is responsible for your information?

Debra Elizabeth Upton, trading as Inner Pathway Counselling, is the data controller responsible for the personal information described in this notice.

Correspondence address: Beacon House, 1 Willow Walk, Skelmersdale, WN8 6UR.
Email: debi@innerpathway.co.uk
Telephone: 07363 056570
ICO registration: ZC188168.

2. Who this notice covers

This notice covers adults aged 18 or over who enquire about counselling, attend an introductory call, receive counselling, contact the practice or choose to receive optional updates.

3. The information we use

Depending on your contact with the practice, information may include your name and contact details; enquiries; initial assessments; GP and emergency contact details; appointment and attendance records; signed counselling agreements; payment records; brief session notes and relevant correspondence.

Counselling may involve information about physical or mental health, wellbeing, disability or neurodivergence. This is special-category personal data requiring additional protection. We record only information that is relevant to the counselling relationship or another lawful purpose.

4. Why we use information and the lawful bases

  • Enquiries, appointments and counselling: steps you request before entering into a counselling contract and performance of that contract (UK GDPR Article 6(1)(b)).
  • Appropriate professional records and responses to complaints or claims: legitimate interests (Article 6(1)(f)), including professional continuity, safeguarding the practice and establishing or defending legal claims where appropriate.
  • Records or disclosures required by law: legal obligation (Article 6(1)(c)), where applicable.
  • Optional marketing communications: consent or another appropriate lawful basis where permitted. If we rely on consent, you may withdraw it at any time without affecting counselling.

For health information needed to provide counselling, the current Admin Privacy Policy identifies Article 9(2)(h) of the UK GDPR and Schedule 1, Part 1, paragraph 2 of the Data Protection Act 2018 (healthcare or treatment), alongside professional confidentiality obligations. Other legal conditions may apply in limited circumstances, for example to establish or defend legal claims.

We do not sell your personal information.

5. Confidentiality, supervision and limited disclosures

Counselling is confidential, subject to the limits described in your counselling agreement. These may include legal requirements, serious safeguarding concerns or immediate safety concerns. Where it is appropriate and safe, Debi will normally discuss a proposed disclosure with you first and share only what is necessary.

Professional clinical supervision may involve discussion of client work. Identifying details are minimised or removed wherever practicable, and the supervisor is subject to professional confidentiality.

6. Who may receive information?

Where necessary and lawful, information may be shared with a confidential clinical supervisor; your GP or emergency contact; relevant emergency, safeguarding or statutory services; courts or public authorities where required; professional indemnity insurers and legal or professional advisers; and accountants or technical service providers with a genuine need for the relevant information. Only the minimum appropriate information is shared.

7. Paper and electronic client records

Client records are held in both paper and electronic form. Paper copies are stored in a lockable cabinet in Debi's counselling office. Electronic copies remain in Debi's access-restricted Clinical area and are not placed in the shared administrative workspace or Practice Manager TEST environment.

Separate encrypted replication and encrypted backups support recovery. The practice limits access and follows retention and secure-disposal procedures. Counselling notes use a client reference code rather than a name wherever practicable, with identifying information kept separately.

8. Business email, website and other services

The website is published through GitHub Pages; it does not have an online enquiry form. Enquiries sent to the published email address are handled through Proton Mail. Approved administrative files may use Proton Drive, but this is not the primary Clinical record store or the clinical backup described above.

GitHub may handle the technical connection and service logs needed to deliver the website. The site does not currently use third-party advertising pixels or analytics trackers. If non-essential tracking is introduced, this notice and any consent arrangements will be reviewed before use.

External links, including the Carer Burnout Check questionnaire and social media profiles, lead to services with their own privacy information. Check the relevant privacy notice before supplying information. Any practice use of responses received from a third-party questionnaire must be explained where relevant.

9. International processing

Some external service providers may process personal or technical information outside the UK. The practice reviews the provider and the applicable UK data-transfer arrangements, including UK adequacy regulations or contractual safeguards where required. You may contact Debi for information about relevant transfers and how to obtain details of safeguards.

10. How long records are kept

  • Enquiries that do not become counselling: normally up to six months after the last meaningful contact.
  • Counselling records: normally five years after the final counselling session. This meets the current professional indemnity insurer’s condition to keep counselling/treatment records and signed agreements or consent records for at least five years from treatment.
  • Financial and accounting records: for the applicable period required by tax and accounting law.
  • Optional marketing: while subscribed, or until consent is withdrawn where applicable; minimal unsubscribe records may be retained.

The UK GDPR does not prescribe a fixed five-year retention period. The practice uses the documented insurer requirement and professional need to justify its period and reviews records when retention is due to end.

Some records may be kept longer if required for a complaint, legal claim, safeguarding concern, insurance matter or other legal obligation. After retention ends, paper records are securely destroyed and electronic copies are securely deleted where reasonably practicable.

11. Your data-protection rights

Depending on the circumstances and lawful basis, you may have rights to access your information, correct inaccuracies, request erasure or restriction, request portability, and withdraw consent where it is the basis for processing. These rights are not absolute and legal or professional retention requirements may apply.

Your right to object: you have the right to object to processing based on legitimate interests, including direct marketing, where applicable. If you object to direct marketing, that processing must stop.

To make a request, contact debi@innerpathway.co.uk. We normally respond within one month.

12. Other useful privacy information

You provide most information directly, although relevant information may sometimes be received lawfully from a GP, emergency contact or other person involved in your care or safety. Basic details needed to arrange safe and suitable counselling are necessary to provide the service; if they are not available, we may be unable to proceed. You can discuss what information is necessary before sharing it.

The practice does not use decisions made solely by automated systems that produce legal or similarly significant effects for clients. General-purpose AI tools are not routinely given identifiable clinical information.

13. Questions, concerns and complaints

Please contact Debi using the email, telephone or correspondence address above if you have a privacy question, want to exercise your rights or have a concern about how information is handled.

You may also complain to the Information Commissioner's Office (ICO), the UK's data protection regulator.
